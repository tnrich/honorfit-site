New Year Reset Challenge

Jan 1–31, 2027: four weeks to scroll less, one small change a week.

Hey y'all! Every January a lot of us promise to quit our phones, delete Instagram and read 30 books. By week three the apps are back and the books aren't. So this year, how about a reset that's a little less dramatic? Four weeks, one small change a week, and the apps that eat your day cost something to open: a few pushups, squats or situps per minute.

I built an app for this after getting tired of filming myself doing pushups at a gas station for one of those camera apps, so I'm biased. But the plan below works with or without it. And if you did No-Scroll November, think of this as round two.

Why not just quit cold turkey?

Deleting every app on January 1st feels great for about four days. Then you reinstall one “just to check something,” and the whole resolution goes with it. A price works better than a ban. You can still open the app, it just costs ten pushups first. Sometimes you pay it and scroll with a clear conscience. Sometimes you look at the lock screen and realize you didn't really want to open it. Both are a win.

The rules

  1. Pick one to three apps. The ones your screen time report says eat your day. Not everything, or you'll quit by day four. Messages, maps and work apps stay free.
  2. Set a price. One pushup for one minute is a good start. If pushups are rough, squats count. So do situps, a walk, or whatever you actually do.
  3. Earn it before you open it. No reps, no scroll.
  4. Log your reps honestly. Nobody's checking. The only person you'd be fooling is you.
  5. Do at least one set a day. Even on days you barely scroll. That's your streak.

The 4-week plan

WeekWhat you do
Jan 1–7
Notice		Write down last week's number from your screen time report. Block just your worst app. Every time you reach for it, notice what you were doing right before.
Jan 8–14
Mornings		No phone for the first 30 minutes of the day. Charge it outside the bedroom, and do your first set before you check anything.
Jan 15–21
Tighten		Add a second app, or make each rep worth a little less time. This is the week resolutions usually fall apart, so expect to want to renegotiate. Keep the price anyway.
Jan 22–31
Evenings		Stop earning time after 9 p.m. What you don't have, you can't scroll in bed. On the 31st, compare your screen time with week one and pick the one rule you're keeping for February.

Doing it without an app

Totally doable. Set a daily limit for your worst app in Screen Time (iPhone) or Digital Wellbeing (Android), keep a sticky note on the fridge with today's reps, and only tap “ignore limit” after you've done a set. For week 2, a cheap alarm clock means the phone can sleep in the kitchen. For week 4, Downtime (iPhone) or Bedtime mode (Android) makes the phone a lot less fun after 9. It's even more honor system than mine, but it works if you stick to it.

FAQ

When is the New Year Reset Challenge?

January 1 to January 31, 2027. Starting late is fine. Just run the four weeks from whatever day you start.

Do I have to quit social media?

No. You pick one to three apps and make them cost a few reps to open. Messages, maps and work apps stay free. The goal is less mindless scrolling, not zero phone.

Do I need an app to do it?

No. A sticky note and your phone's Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing limits are enough if you stick to them. An exercise app blocker like HonorFit just makes it automatic: the apps lock themselves, and you unlock them by logging reps.

What if I miss a day?

Start again the next morning. A reset is about spending less of January scrolling, not about a perfect record.

What happens on February 1?

Keep the one rule that made the biggest difference and drop the rest. One habit that lasts beats four that don't.
