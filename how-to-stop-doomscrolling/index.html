How to stop doomscrolling

Without deleting every app and reinstalling it four days later.

Doomscrolling isn’t a discipline problem, which is why treating it like one keeps failing. The feed is a machine built by very good engineers to find the exact thing that will keep your thumb moving, and it retrains on you every session. Trying to out-willpower it at 11pm, tired, is not a fair fight and you already know how it ends.

What works is changing the terms so you don’t have to win that fight.

1. Notice which app it actually is

Most people are wrong about this. Check your own screen time report before you do anything else — the fix for a two-hour YouTube habit is different from the fix for forty pickups of Instagram a day. Block the one that shows up, not the one you feel guilty about.

2. Stop relying on a limit you can dismiss

Built-in app timers fail for a structural reason: the “ignore limit” button is right there, and pressing it costs nothing. After about a week it becomes muscle memory — you’re past the screen before you’ve consciously decided anything. Any block whose escape hatch is one tap eventually becomes a block with one extra tap.

3. Attack the trigger, not the session

Almost every scroll starts the same way: an idle second, a hand reaching for the phone, an icon in the same spot it’s always been. Cheap, boring changes to that path do real work — move the app off the home screen and out of the dock, turn off every non-human notification, and charge the phone in another room overnight. None of this is dramatic. It removes the automatic part, which is most of it.

4. Make opening the app cost something

This is the piece the other tactics are missing. Friction only holds if the price is real, and the most reliable real price is physical. Twenty pushups to unlock ten minutes of Instagram is not a punishment — it’s an exchange rate. Some of the time you’ll pay it, and that’s fine. The rest of the time you’ll put the phone down, because it turns out you didn’t want it enough to get on the floor.

Either outcome is a win, which is why it beats a blocker that only ever produces frustration. You’re not trying to never open Instagram. You’re trying to make opening it a decision instead of a reflex.

5. Spend the earned time on purpose

If your earned minutes drain while the phone is face-down on a table, you’ll stop trusting the system. Time should only be spent while you’re actually in the app. Then a balance means what it says, and running out means something.

6. Give it two weeks, and expect to renegotiate

The first few days are uncomfortable and you’ll reach for the phone constantly out of habit. That fades. What usually needs adjusting is the exchange rate: set it too harsh and you’ll uninstall the blocker; set it too generous and nothing changes. Start somewhere reasonable — a few minutes per rep — and tune it after the first week against how you actually behaved.
