Using an app blocker when you have ADHD

Practical notes, not medical advice. HonorFit is a tool, not a treatment.

Quick note first: I'm an app developer, not a doctor, and nothing on this page is medical advice. HonorFit doesn't treat ADHD or anything else. If ADHD is getting in the way of your life, a doctor or another qualified professional is the right person to talk to.

What I can share is the practical side: why a lot of app blockers don't stick, what some people with ADHD find works better, and how to set one up so you don't delete it by Friday.

Why app blockers often don't stick

  • Too easy to skip. A limit with an “Ignore” button becomes a reflex. After a week your thumb taps it before you've noticed.
  • Too strict. A hard wall that blocks something you actually need, like messages or a work app, gets turned off the first time it's in the way.
  • Too much setup. If getting back into an app takes a ritual, the blocker is the first thing to go on a busy day.
  • The novelty wears off. The first week is exciting. By week three it's just another rule.

What some people find helpful

  • A small, real cost instead of a ban. Some people with ADHD find a quick pause or a small physical task interrupts the automatic reach for the phone better than a timer they can dismiss.
  • Something you can see. If losing track of time is your thing, a visible countdown makes the time feel real.
  • Always a way back in. A blocker that lets you earn your way back is less likely to get deleted in frustration than one that just says no.
  • Getting up. Some people like that the price of a scroll is a few pushups or squats, because it breaks the loop and gets them off the couch for a second.
  • Starting small. One or two apps, not your whole phone. You can always add more later.

The main kinds of blockers

There's no single best one. It depends on what trips you up:

  • A pause before the app opens: one sec and ScreenZen. Gentle, quick, good for breaking the autopilot.
  • Schedules and focus sessions: Opal, or your phone's built-in Downtime and Focus modes. Good if your problem is specific hours.
  • A physical key: Brick, a device you tap to block and unblock. Good if you need a hard stop.
  • Exercise to unlock: HonorFit (honor system) and Pushscroll (counts reps with the camera). Good if you want the cost to be physical.

How HonorFit works

You pick the apps to block. They stay locked until you log some reps on a wheel: pushups, situps, squats or pull-ups. By default, a pushup earns a minute and a squat earns 30 seconds. Your time goes into one balance that only counts down while a blocked app is on screen, and while your apps are unlocked, a quiet notification counts down the time you have left. When it hits zero, the apps lock again.

There's no camera to set up, so going from “I want Instagram” to logging ten squats takes seconds. That matters if setup friction is what usually kills your habits. And the free version caps your banked time at 20 minutes, so you can't stockpile a whole evening of scrolling in one burst of motivation.

Setup tips

  • Block one app first. Whichever one your screen time report says, not the one you feel guiltiest about.
  • Leave the defaults alone for a week. Then adjust. Too harsh and you'll quit; too generous and nothing changes.
  • Use squats when the floor isn't an option. They're easy to do anywhere.
  • Plan for off days. Pro has a pause mode for days you need your phone, a daily baseline that starts each day with some free minutes, and hard mode if it's getting too easy.

What it won't do

HonorFit won't treat anything, and it won't stop you if you really want in. It runs on the honor system, so you can log reps you didn't do, and like any app blocker, you can turn it off. It also only locks apps, not websites. It's a tool that puts a small price on scrolling. For some people that's exactly enough; for others, something stricter works better.

FAQ

Is HonorFit an ADHD app?

No. It's a general app blocker, not a medical or ADHD-specific app, and it doesn't treat ADHD. Some people with ADHD find exercise-to-unlock friction useful, but it's a tool, not a treatment.

What kind of app blocker is best for ADHD?

There isn't one answer. Some people do best with a gentle pause (one sec, ScreenZen), some with schedules (Opal or built-in Downtime), some with a physical device (Brick), and some with exercise to unlock (HonorFit, Pushscroll). Give one at least two weeks and adjust its settings before switching.

Will doing pushups to unlock my phone help my ADHD?

I can't promise that, and HonorFit doesn't make health claims. What it does is put a small physical cost between you and the apps you choose. If you have questions about ADHD and exercise, ask a doctor.

What if I need my phone for something important?

HonorFit only locks the apps you pick. Everything else keeps working normally, so leave messaging or work apps off the list if you need them. Pro also has a pause mode that turns blocking off for a while and resumes on its own.

Does HonorFit collect data about me?

No. There's no account and no analytics, and nothing leaves your phone. On iPhone, Apple doesn't even tell HonorFit which apps you picked.
